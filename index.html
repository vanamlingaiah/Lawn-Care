Seasonal mowing guide
❄️ Dormant Season
December – February
No mowing needed. Grass is dormant. Do not scalp — brown turf protects roots from hard freezes common in Denton County.
▸ Use this time to sharpen blade, change oil, service equipment.
🌤 Wake-Up Season
March – April
Mow once grass reaches 4". Frequency: every 10–14 days to start. Height: 3". First mow: bag clippings to remove thatch and dead material.
▸ Do not fertilize until grass is 50% green and actively growing.
☀️ Active Growth
May – June · September
Mow weekly at 3–3.5". Mulch clippings back into lawn for free nitrogen. Alternate mowing direction each week to avoid ruts and stand grass upright.
▸ May and September are the best months for edging all beds and curbs.
🔥 Peak Heat Season
July – August
Raise deck to 3.5". Mow only if grass is actively growing — skip weeks if needed. Taller grass shades roots from Denton County's intense summer heat. Never mow wilted grass.
▸ Mow before 9am. Deep water the day before mowing in dry spells.
🍂 Wind-Down Season
October – November
Every 2–3 weeks as growth slows. Final mow in mid-November at 2.5" to reduce leaf smothering and disease risk. Bag clippings on the last mow of the year.
▸ Mulch light leaf fall; bag heavy leaf accumulation before dormancy.

BEDS & GARDEN
Mulch (Hardwood or Cedar)
April — refresh annually
3" layer in all beds retains moisture, suppresses weeds, and regulates soil temp. Cedar repels insects. Refresh every spring. Pull mulch away from plant bases.
❄️
Winterization Guide
Aubrey first frost: typically mid-Nov. Begin October — don't wait for freeze warnings.
🍂 October — Active Prep
Before soil temps drop below 55°F
Apply Scotts Winterizer Turf Builder — most important application of the year
Roundup spot treat all fall annual weeds before they go to seed
Core aerate if due (every 2 years) — do before winterizer for best absorption
Final edging of season — beds, driveway, walkways
Raise mow deck to 3.5" for final mows — freeze protection for roots
Refresh mulch in beds (3" depth) to insulate plant roots for winter
🚿 Irrigation Shutoff
Mid-November — before first freeze forecast
Turn off irrigation controller / smart timer
Shut off irrigation main supply valve at backflow preventer
Blow out all irrigation lines with compressor (hire a tech if needed)
Insulate exposed backflow preventer with foam wrap
Drain and store hoses indoors or coil flat in garage
✂️ Final Mow
Mid-November — after most growth stops
Final mow at 2.5–3" height — reduces leaf smothering risk
Bag all clippings and leaf debris on final mow
Rake and clear any heavy leaf accumulation from lawn
🛠️ Equipment Storage
After last mow — before storing for winter
Add fuel stabilizer to mower gas tank or run completely dry
Sharpen and clean mower blade — ready for spring first use
Change mower oil if due (every 50 hrs or once a year)
Clean fertilizer spreader thoroughly — residue corrodes the frame
Wipe and oil edger blade and string trimmer head
Store all tools in dry covered area — garage or shed
🌑 During Dormancy (Dec–Feb)
What to do and what to avoid
Do NOT fertilize during dormancy — damages stressed root system
Water lightly if no rain for 3+ weeks (prevent drought dormancy)
Avoid heavy foot traffic on frozen or frost-covered grass
Check irrigation for leaks after first thaw in late winter
Plan next year — restock products in January for best selection
